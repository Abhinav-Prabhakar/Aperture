Calibration & QC Panel

A calibration line is a claim that the instrument still measures what it says it measures. This panel puts the standards, the blanks, the limits, and the control chart next to a verdict, so a failed run cannot be released by accident.

Six-point external standard curve, 1/x weighting not applied; blank SD from five independent blanks. Control limits from the 2026 stable period.

Six standards, five blanks, ten weekly controls. The curve is excellent — r² rounds to 1.0000 — but the 19 October control sits 4 SD above the mean, so the 1-3s rule fires and the verdict is Reject. That is the point: a good line does not rescue a drifting control.

What it checks

  • The fit is reported, not hidden. Slope, intercept, r², predicted values, and residuals for every standard. The acceptance threshold is printed next to the verdict.
  • LOD and LOQ come from the blanks. 3.3 × SD / |slope| and 10 × SD / |slope|, with the blank count and SD shown. Without blanks, the limits are marked unestablished rather than guessed.
  • Control rules are named. 1-3s, 2-2s, R-4s, 4-1s, and 10-x. Each violation lists the rule, the date, the value, and a plain-language reason.
  • The verdict combines curve and controls. A failed r² or any Westgard rejection rejects the run. A CV or bias above the limit, or missing blanks, moves it to review.
  • The chart marks the failures. Control points that violate a rule are ringed and crossed. The rest of the series stays visible; hiding outliers is how drift gets missed.
  • The CSV carries the evidence. Standards with predicted values and residuals, blanks, and controls, one row each.

How the fit works

  • Ordinary least squares on level versus response. No weighting, no forced zero, no outlier removal. If the method needs 1/x weighting, it needs a different fit and a domain review.
  • r² is computed from the residuals against the mean response. A flat response gives r² 0 rather than a division by zero.
  • Controls use the target mean and SD when provided. Without them, the observed mean and sample SD of the plotted points are used, which is a demo convenience, not a validated control limit.
  • Bias needs a target. Without controlMean, the panel does not invent a bias.

Data contract

  1. Standards. Level, response, and optional one-sigma response uncertainty. Fewer than two usable standards renders the empty state.
  2. Blanks and controls. Blanks are response values. Controls are { at, value, runId? }, in chronological order.
  3. Options. minRSquared (0.995), maxCv (5%), and maxBias (5%).
  4. Helpers. fitLinearCalibration, calibrationLimits, evaluateWestgard, summarizeCalibrationQc, and calibrationCsv are exported.

Accessibility

  • Both charts are role="img" with an accessible label that states the fit, the limits, and the violation count. The same numbers are in the definition list and the standards table.
  • The verdict is a word and an icon, not a colored bar. The reasons are a sentence.
  • Violations are text rows, not only marks on the chart.
  • The layout stacks the chart, statistics, and standards table at 320 px inside the component; the page does not scroll sideways.

Preview status

The fit, the limits, the rules, and the export are complete and tested against reference series. The defaults still need a domain-advisor review: linear versus weighted fit, blank replicates versus a pooled SD, the acceptance thresholds, and the exact Westgard rule set used by a given laboratory. Treat this instrument as usable, not final. The Measurement Table and Reproducibility Manifest carry Ready status.
